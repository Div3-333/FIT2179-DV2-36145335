**Moodle Response (443 words)**

Domain and audience: *From Sky to Tap* examines Victorian water security under compounding climate stress: rainfall deficits, warming, evaporation, reservoir response, groundwater pressure and desalination. It is designed for a general Australian audience, not hydrologists: the goal is to make the system understandable to residents who know drought and water restrictions but may not understand how meteorological stress becomes infrastructure stress.

Why: The visualisation addresses a public communication problem. Climate and water data are usually published as separate technical datasets, which makes it difficult to see the sequence from weather anomaly to supply response. The project therefore asks: where is rainfall stress concentrated, how has heat shifted through time, how do climate drivers and evaporation intensify catchment pressure, and when does engineered supply become significant? The story argues that Victoria’s water security is still fundamentally rain-dependent, but desalination can operate as a stabilising buffer during low-storage periods.

What: The data combines multiple reliable public sources: Bureau of Meteorology rainfall, ACORN-SAT temperature and Southern Oscillation Index records; SILO evaporation, rainfall and temperature data; Melbourne Water reservoir storage; Victorian reservoir capacity/location data; groundwater bore-permit density; Victorian Water Accounts resource-use data; and desalination/demand records from Victorian water planning documents. Derived data were used only to clarify structure: monthly rainfall anomalies, two historical temperature windows, an OLS evaporation trendline, normalised water-resource shares, desalination dependency ratios, spatial interpolation/grid density, and storage-desalination state positions.

Why tasks in Munzner’s framework: users are guided to discover spatial patterns, compare historical and modern periods, identify temporal trends and anomalies, relate climate signals to local rainfall, compare reservoir magnitude against percentage fullness, and summarise system state through final engineered-supply views. The intended task is not expert exploration; it is guided presentation with enough interaction to test the narrative.

How: The design uses Vega-Lite maps and diagrams embedded in a single scrolling narrative. Spatial marks locate rainfall deficit, reservoir capacity and groundwater stress. Temporal heatmaps, line charts, area charts and bar charts expose change over time. Position encodes ordered quantitative values; colour is used sparingly for meaningful categories or intensity; size encodes reservoir capacity and rainfall magnitude where comparison benefits from magnitude cues. Annotations, arrows, tooltips and colour-coded textual terms connect visual evidence to prose without forcing the viewer to infer everything unaided. The page follows a logical Munzner What/Why/How progression: sky/climate drivers, catchment response, hidden groundwater buffer, then engineered survival.

Special features include linked toggle states across the final three charts, normalised proportional composition, a bivariate survival matrix, and a methodology/audit section that documents sources, transformations and idiom rationale. Overall, the project transforms fragmented hydrological and climate records into a coherent explanatory story about Victoria’s transition from rainfall reliance toward conditional engineered resilience.
